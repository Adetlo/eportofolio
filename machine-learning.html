Collaborative Discussion: The 4th Industrial Revolution

Posted by Ade Putra Tio Aldino - Monday, 11 August 2025, 4:19 PM

Industry 4.0 ushered in cyber-physical systems, IoT, big data, and AI, revolutionizing factories with instant monitoring, smart maintenance planning, and streamlined supply chains (Lu, 2017). As we advance to Industry 5.0, the focus is on blending human-centered design, resilience, and ecological responsibility, fostering seamless teamwork between skilled workers and intelligent machines (European Commission, 2021).

A vivid illustration comes from the LockerGoga incident that hit Norsk Hydro, a giant in the aluminium sector. The malware severely disrupted its international networks, leaving the firm with no option but to revert to paper and spreadsheets in dozens of plants (Greenberg, 2019).

Key Impacts Identified:

  • Customer & operational disruption: Production came to a standstill or progressed at reduced speed, compounding delivery backlogs and eroding customer trust.
  • Economic cost: Preliminary estimates put revenue and remediation losses at USD 75–100 million (Greenberg, 2019).
  • Reputational cost: Coverage by national and financial press exposed soft spots in essential industrial systems, diminishing confidence among regulators and investors.
  • Additional fallout: Elevated stress among workers, intensified pressure on crisis response squads, and a sprint toward stronger cyber-defensive spending.

This episode spotlights Industry 4.0's paradox: digital linking boosts productivity yet exposes latent weak points. Transitioning to Industry 5.0 requires design that prioritizes resilience, retains effective human oversight, and operates within a clear ethical framework to close the gaps revealed by this breach.

References

  • European Commission (2021) Industry 5.0: Towards a sustainable, human-centric and resilient European industry. Brussels: European Commission.
  • Greenberg, A. (2019) 'Aluminum Producer Norsk Hydro Still Reeling From LockerGoga Ransomware Attack', Wired, 19 March.
  • Lu, Y. (2017) 'Industry 4.0: A survey on technologies, applications and open research issues', Journal of Industrial Information Integration, 6, pp. 1–10.

E-Portfolio Activity: Gradient Descent Cost Function

Following the Mayo (2017) article, I implemented and experimented with gradient descent:

Experiment 1: Varying Learning Rate

  • LR = 0.001 (Conservative): Slow but steady, required 5000+ iterations
  • LR = 0.1 (Aggressive): Rapid initial decrease, oscillation after ~500 iterations
  • LR = 1.0 (Too High): Divergence observed—cost increased rather than decreased
  • Optimal LR = 0.01-0.05: Balanced speed and stability

Experiment 2: Varying Iteration Count

  • 100 Iterations: Insufficient—network barely started learning
  • 500 Iterations: Moderate improvement, cost still decreasing
  • 1000 Iterations: Good convergence for simple problems
  • 5000 Iterations: Diminishing returns after 2000 iterations

Key Insight: Early stopping based on validation error is more efficient than fixed iterations.

Weight Update Rule:

new_weight = old_weight - (learning_rate × gradient)

CNNs have revolutionized computer vision, enabling applications from medical imaging to autonomous vehicles. This unit explored both the technical foundations and ethical considerations of deploying CNN-based systems.

Ethical Analysis: Computer Vision Applications

Critical Ethical Issues:

  • Surveillance & Privacy: Real-time facial recognition in public spaces raises questions about consent and privacy rights
  • Algorithmic Bias: Studies show facial recognition systems have significantly higher error rates for underrepresented groups (Buolamwini & Gebru, 2018)
  • Deepfakes: GANs creating synthetic media for misinformation and personal harm
  • Autonomous Systems: Accountability questions when CNN-based systems make errors

Positive Applications:

  • Healthcare: Early disease detection in medical imaging (cancer screening, diabetic retinopathy)
  • Accessibility: Computer vision assists visually impaired individuals in navigation
  • Environmental: Wildlife monitoring, deforestation detection from satellite imagery
  • Safety: Finding missing persons, threat detection in security screening

Recommendations for Responsible CNN Deployment:

  • Establish clear legal boundaries for facial recognition in public spaces
  • Develop diverse, representative training datasets to reduce bias
  • Implement bias testing and mitigation throughout development lifecycle
  • Create explainable AI (XAI) methods for CNN decision transparency

This unit explored Natural Language Processing (NLP), one of the most rapidly evolving fields in machine learning. The advent of Transformer-based architectures has revolutionized language understanding.

Understanding Transformer Architectures

Transformer Breakthrough (Vaswani et al., 2017):

  • Self-Attention: Allows model to weigh importance of different words regardless of distance
  • Parallel Processing: All positions processed simultaneously, dramatically reducing training time
  • Positional Encoding: Maintains sequence information without sequential processing
  • Multi-Head Attention: Captures different types of relationships simultaneously

BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations):

  • Architecture: Encoder-only, bidirectional context understanding
  • Training: Masked Language Modeling (MLM)
  • Applications: Question answering, sentiment analysis, NER
  • Strength: Excellent for understanding tasks

GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer):

  • Architecture: Decoder-only, unidirectional processing
  • Training: Autoregressive language modeling
  • Applications: Text generation, creative writing, code generation
  • Strength: Exceptional generation capabilities, few-shot learning

T5 (Text-to-Text Transfer Transformer):

  • Unified Framework: All NLP tasks framed as text-to-text problems
  • Architecture: Encoder-decoder structure
  • Applications: Translation, summarization, question answering

NLP Evaluation Metrics

  • BLEU: N-gram overlap for machine translation (0-100, higher is better)
  • ROUGE: Recall-oriented for summarization tasks
  • Perplexity: Measures how well model predicts text (lower is better)

MLOps Applications in NLP:

  • Data drift detection for language models
  • Automated retraining pipelines
  • A/B testing for model deployment

Why Transfer Learning?

  • Challenge: Small images (32×32) with limited spatial information
  • Risk: 50K training samples risk overfitting when training from scratch
  • Solution: Reuse ImageNet pretrained knowledge (1.2M images)
  • Result: +15% better accuracy vs training from scratch

MobileNetV2 Architecture

TensorFlow Keras MobileNetV2 Transfer Learning Grad-CAM

Architecture Stack:

  • Input: 160×160×3 (resized from 32×32)
  • Pretrained MobileNetV2 feature extractor
  • Global Average Pooling
  • Dense Layer (128 units, ReLU)
  • Dropout (0.3)
  • Output (10 units, Softmax)

Two-Phase Training Strategy

Phase 1 (5 epochs):

  • Only classifier head trainable
  • Learning Rate: 1e-3
  • Purpose: Warm-up to avoid catastrophic forgetting

Phase 2 (15 epochs):

  • Last 30% of backbone unfrozen
  • Learning Rate: 1e-4 (reduced for fine-tuning)
  • Result: +3-5% accuracy improvement

Performance Results

Overall Metrics:

  • Test Accuracy: 94.46%
  • Macro Precision: 0.945
  • Macro Recall: 0.944
  • Macro F1-Score: 0.944

Best Performing Classes:

  • Automobile: F1 = 0.9683
  • Ship: F1 = 0.9603
  • Truck: F1 = 0.9563
  • Airplane: F1 = 0.9523

Challenging Classes:

  • Cat: F1 = 0.8813 (confusion with dog due to similar textures)
  • Dog: F1 = 0.9082 (missed some true positives)

Explainability with Grad-CAM

Implemented Grad-CAM (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping) to visualize which image regions influence predictions:

  • Verified model focuses on relevant object features, not background
  • Demonstrated meaningful learned representations
  • Aligns with EU AI Act requirements for explainability

This unit completed a full circle from Unit 1, connecting the Norsk Hydro ransomware case to Industry 5.0's emphasis on resilience and human-centricity as frameworks for addressing Industry 4.0's challenges.

Industry 5.0 - Three Pillars

1. Human-Centricity:

  • Workers and machines collaborate seamlessly
  • Technology adapts to human needs
  • Focus on worker wellbeing and augmentation

2. Sustainability:

  • Circular economy principles
  • Energy efficiency and renewable integration
  • ML-driven resource management

3. Resilience:

  • Adaptive systems that handle disruptions
  • Diversified supply chains
  • Embedded cybersecurity with graceful degradation

Future Trends in Machine Learning

  • Self-Supervised Learning (SSL): Models learn from unlabeled data, democratizing AI development
  • Edge AI: ML inference on edge devices—our MobileNetV2 project exemplifies these principles
  • Neural Architecture Search (NAS): Automated network design using ML itself
  • Federated Learning: Training without centralizing data for privacy preservation
  • Explainable AI (XAI): LIME, SHAP, Grad-CAM for regulatory compliance
  • Multimodal Learning: Processing text, images, audio simultaneously

Emerging Roles in Industry 4.0/5.0

  • Machine Learning Engineer: Designing, training, and deploying ML models
  • Data Scientist: Extracting insights from complex datasets
  • AI Ethics Specialist: Ensuring responsible AI development
  • MLOps Engineer: Managing ML model lifecycle in production
  • Digital Twin Specialist: Creating virtual replicas of physical systems
  • Edge AI Developer: Optimizing models for resource-constrained devices

This unit completes a full circle from Unit 1, where I discussed the Norsk Hydro ransomware attack exemplifying Industry 4.0's paradox—digital connectivity enables unprecedented efficiency yet creates critical vulnerabilities.
